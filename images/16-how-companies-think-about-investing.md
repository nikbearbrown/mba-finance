# Image briefs — Chapter 16

## Figure 16.1 — NPV profile

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** A line chart showing NPV as a function of discount rate for Sam's project. NPV is high at low rates, declines as the rate rises, crosses zero at the IRR (~14%), and goes negative beyond. Line annotated with the cost of capital line at 9% (showing positive NPV).

**Caption.** *NPV declines as discount rate rises. The IRR is the rate at which NPV = 0. The cost of capital is your hurdle.*

---

## Figure 16.2 — Multiple IRRs

**Where:** Concept 1, in the IRR section.

**Subject.** NPV profile for the Olympic facility example. The curve is non-monotonic — it rises, then falls, then rises again — crossing zero twice. Two IRRs labeled.

**Caption.** *When cash flows change sign more than once, the NPV profile can cross zero twice. Multiple IRRs. The decision rule fails.*

---

## Figure 16.3 — Five metrics compared

**Where:** Concept 1 conclusion.

**Subject.** Table comparing the five metrics: each row a metric, columns showing strengths, weaknesses, and primary use case. Color-coded by recommendation (NPV in green, payback in yellow, etc.).

**Caption.** *The toolkit. NPV is the gold standard; the others are supplementary or special-case tools.*

---

## Figure 16.4 — Profitability index ranking

**Where:** Concept 2.

**Subject.** Bar chart of PI ranking for a set of projects. Cumulative budget consumed at the bottom; the chart visually shows which projects fit within the budget.

**Caption.** *Capital rationing. Rank by PI; select until budget is exhausted; total NPV is maximized.*
