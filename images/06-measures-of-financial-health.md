# Image briefs — Chapter 6: Measures of Financial Health

## Figure 6.1 — Same number, three ratios

**Where:** Hook section.

**Subject.** Two side-by-side panels: Firm A (small specialty retailer with $100M revenue, $80M assets, $30M NI) and Firm B (large industrial with $50B revenue, $80B assets, $30M NI). Below each, the same three ratios computed: profit margin, ROA, asset turnover. Color-coded to highlight that identical net income produces wildly different ratios.

**Caption.** *Same net income, opposite stories. Ratios are how analysts make the comparison meaningful.*

## Figure 6.2 — The five families of ratios

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** A 2x3 grid (or 5-circle diagram) showing the five families: Efficiency, Liquidity, Solvency, Market Value, Profitability. Each cell contains 2-3 example ratios and a one-line description of what the family measures.

**Caption.** *Five families, one connected diagnostic system. Each measures a different dimension of financial health.*

---

## Figure 6.3 — DuPont decomposition

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** Visual breakdown showing ROE = Profit Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier. Each component labeled with what it measures (operating efficiency, asset efficiency, financial leverage). Arrows showing how each can independently change ROE.

**Caption.** *Three drivers, one number. DuPont decomposition surfaces what's actually changing inside ROE.*

## Figure 6.4 — Industry ROE comparison (2020 snapshot)

**Where:** Concept 3, in the industry context section.

**Subject.** Horizontal bar chart of industry ROEs (selected industries from the OpenStax data table). Color-coded: positive in green, negative in red. Total market average shown as a vertical reference line.

**Caption.** *ROE varies dramatically by industry. A 13% ROE means very different things in software (below average) vs. recreational products (above average).*
