# Image briefs — Chapter 10: Bonds

## Figure 10.1 — Inverse relationship: bond prices and yields

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** Curve showing bond price as a function of YTM, for a representative 10-year coupon bond. Premium region (price > par) at low yields; discount region (price < par) at high yields; crossover at coupon rate.

**Caption.** *The inverse relationship is the central mechanic of bond pricing. When yields fall below the coupon rate, the bond becomes more attractive than alternatives, and price rises above par.*

---

## Figure 10.2 — Two cash flow streams

**Where:** Concept 2, opening.

**Subject.** Timeline showing a 10-year bond's cash flows: small constant coupon payments (years 1-9) plus a large coupon-plus-par payment (year 10). Color-coded: coupon stream in one color, par in another.

**Caption.** *A bond's cash flows are an annuity (the coupons) plus a single payment (the par value at maturity). Bond pricing is the sum of the present values of both.*

---

## Figure 10.3 — Yield curve shapes

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** Three small panels showing typical yield curve shapes: normal (upward sloping), inverted (downward sloping), flat. Annotated with typical economic interpretations.

**Caption.** *The yield curve carries macro information. Normal in expansion; inverted before recessions (probabilistically); flat in transitions.*

---

## Figure 10.4 — Bond rating ladder

**Where:** Concept 3, in the ratings section.

**Subject.** Vertical bar showing S&P/Fitch ratings (AAA at top, D at bottom), with the BBB/BB boundary highlighted as the investment-grade vs. junk line. Moody's notation shown alongside.

**Caption.** *Investment grade ends at BBB/Baa. Below that line, "junk" or "high-yield" — higher risk, higher demanded yield.*
