## Figure 4.1 — Chris's August: cash vs. accrual side-by-side

**Where:** Concept 1, after the worked example.

**Subject.** Two parallel income statements for Chris's Landscaping in August. Cash-basis on left ($1,400 revenue, $1,150 expenses, $250 net income). Accrual-basis on right ($1,400 revenue, $233 expenses, $1,167 net income). Color-coded annotations showing which transaction landed where.

## Figure 4.2 — The accounting equation as a balance scale

**Where:** Concept 2, opening.

**Subject.** A balance scale visual: assets on the left pan; liabilities + equity on the right pan. Both pans level. Below the scale, three example transactions showing how each side adjusts to keep the scale balanced.

**Caption.** *The accounting equation is a definitional identity. Every transaction adjusts both sides; the scale never tips.*

## Figure 4.3 — Three depreciation methods on the same asset

**Where:** Concept 3, after the depreciation methods section.

**Subject.** Line chart with x-axis = years 1 through 5, y-axis = depreciation expense. Three lines:
- Straight-line: flat at $9,600/year.
- Units-of-production: variable based on usage (sample data given).
- Double-declining-balance: high in year 1 ($23,200), declining each year.

A second panel below shows cumulative depreciation across the five years for each method, all converging at $48,000 at the end.

**Caption.** *Same asset, same total depreciation over five years. Different methods produce dramatically different reported earnings each year.*

## Figure 4.4 — Where revenue lives in the accounting equation

**Where:** Concept 3, in the multi-period revenue section.

**Subject.** A flow diagram showing what happens when a SaaS firm receives $360K cash for a three-year contract:
1. Cash (asset) goes up by $360K.
2. Deferred Revenue (liability) goes up by $360K.
3. Each subsequent month: Deferred Revenue decreases by $10K; Service Revenue (income statement) increases by $10K.

Total flow shown over the three-year contract life.

**Caption.** *Cash arrives once. Revenue is recognized over thirty-six months. Deferred revenue holds the difference on the balance sheet as a liability the firm owes the customer in service.*

## Figure 4.5 — Apple's PP&E from a recent 10-K

**Where:** Concept 3, in the worked example.

**Subject.** A simplified version of Apple's PP&E note: gross PP&E (~$103B) → less accumulated depreciation (~$67B) → net PP&E (~$37B). With breakdown by category (land/buildings, machinery, leasehold improvements, etc.).

**Caption.** *Real-scale depreciation. Apple has $103B of fixed assets at historical cost; two-thirds has been depreciated; the remaining $37B is the book value supporting current operations.*
