## Figure 1.1 — The three areas of finance, as overlapping regions

**Where:** End of Concept 1, after the three-jobs-in-one-word section.

**Subject.** A Venn-style diagram with three large circles labeled *Business Finance*, *Investments*, and *Financial Markets and Institutions*. The overlaps are labeled with examples (e.g., bank balance-sheet decisions sit in the intersection of business finance and markets/institutions; mutual fund management sits in the intersection of investments and markets/institutions).

**Style.** Clean, two-tone. Not cute. Each region carries a one-line example, not a definition.

**Caption.** *Finance divides into three areas, but most real jobs sit in the overlaps. The labels mark the question being asked, not the people answering it.*

## Figure 1.2 — The three business-finance decisions, mapped to the balance sheet

**Where:** Inside Concept 1, in the section *"The three decisions inside business finance."*

**Subject.** A simplified balance-sheet T-account. Three colored regions:
- **Working-capital management** — top of both columns (current assets, current liabilities). Light blue.
- **Capital budgeting** — bottom-left (long-term assets). Light green.
- **Capital structure** — bottom-right (long-term debt + equity). Light orange.

Labels point to which CFO/treasurer/controller decision corresponds to each region.

**Caption.** *Three decisions, three regions. Every job inside corporate finance eventually maps to one of these regions of the balance sheet.*

## Figure 1.4 — Primary vs. secondary market trace

**Where:** Inside Concept 3, after the worked example on tapping "Buy 1 share of AAPL."

**Subject.** Two parallel flow arrows:
1. **Primary market.** Investor → Underwriter → Issuing Firm. Cash flows to the firm. Used in IPO/SEO.
2. **Secondary market.** Investor A → Broker/Dealer → Investor B. Cash flows between investors only. Issuing firm gets nothing.

A small "information arrow" goes from secondary market back to primary market, labeled *"price discovery feeds future issuance."*

**Caption.** *Primary-market trades fund firms. Secondary-market trades fund nothing — but the prices they generate change what firms can raise next time.*

## Figure 1.5 — Brokers, dealers, intermediaries

**Where:** Inside Concept 3, in the *"Players"* section.

**Subject.** Three side-by-side panels, each showing a stylized version of the role:
1. **Broker.** Two clients shown; broker between them with a "$0.00 inventory" tag and a "fee" arrow.
2. **Dealer.** One client; dealer holds an "inventory box" of the security; bid and ask quotes shown.
3. **Intermediary.** Multiple depositors flowing money in; multiple borrowers receiving loans flowing out; the intermediary's balance sheet shown briefly to illustrate transformation.

**Caption.** *Three roles that look similar in passing and behave differently when something goes wrong.*
