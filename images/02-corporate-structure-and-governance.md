# Image briefs — Chapter 2: Corporate Structure and Governance

## Figure 2.1 — Comparison of business forms (table-figure)

**Where:** End of Concept 1, after the four-forms walkthrough.

**Subject.** A clean comparison table — rows: Sole Proprietorship, Partnership, LLC/LLP, C Corporation, S Corporation. Columns: Liability protection, Taxation (entity / pass-through / double), Owner limit, Capital access, Lifetime, Administrative complexity. Color-coded cells (red = disadvantage, green = advantage, yellow = neutral).

**Caption.** *Each form trades liability protection, tax efficiency, and governance simplicity for capital access. There is no dominant form.*

---

## Figure 2.2 — The principal-agent gap

**Where:** Beginning of Concept 2.

**Subject.** A diagram showing two parallel actors: Principal (shareholder, owner) on one side, Agent (manager, executive) on the other. Arrows between them: principal delegates authority and capital; agent provides effort and information. A dashed asymmetry line shows the information gap. Three arrows labeled "agency cost" point downward from the gap.

**Caption.** *The corporate form's central trade-off. Separation enables scale; separation creates the gap.*

---

## Figure 2.3 — The growth arc of a firm's legal form

**Where:** Inside Concept 1, in the worked example.

**Subject.** A horizontal timeline with five labeled milestones (sole prop → 2-member LLC → multi-member LLC → C corp Series A → IPO). Below each milestone, the structural reason for the change.

**Caption.** *Form follows function. Each transition was forced by a capital event the prior structure couldn't accommodate.*
