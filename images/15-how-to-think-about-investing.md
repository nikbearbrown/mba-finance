# Image briefs — Chapter 15

## Figure 15.1 — Time horizon and allocation

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** Stacked-area chart showing recommended equity allocation as a function of time horizon, from 0 years (100% cash) to 30+ years (90% equity). Labeled glide-path style.

**Caption.** *Time horizon drives asset allocation. Short horizons cannot tolerate equity variance.*

---

## Figure 15.2 — Diversification benefit

**Where:** Concept 2.

**Subject.** Curve showing portfolio standard deviation as a function of number of stocks (1 to 50). Steep decline from 1-12; flatter from 12-30; nearly flat thereafter. Annotated with the Statman ~12-stock observation.

**Caption.** *Diversification benefit has diminishing returns. Most of the gain happens within the first dozen uncorrelated holdings.*

---

## Figure 15.3 — Active manager underperformance

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** Bar chart showing % of active US large-cap mutual funds underperforming their benchmark over different time horizons (1, 3, 5, 10, 15, 20 years). Bars rise as horizon lengthens, approaching 90% at 20 years.

**Caption.** *Most active managers underperform their benchmark over long periods. The longer the horizon, the more reliably indexing wins.*

## Figure 15.4 — The fee-compounding chart

**Where:** Concept 3 worked example.

**Subject.** Line chart showing the wealth gap between two investors over 40 years — one paying 1% in fees, one paying 0.05%. Same gross returns; the fee-paying investor finishes about 25% behind.

**Caption.** *Fees compound. A 1% fee over 40 years costs roughly 25% of the indexed investor's final wealth.*
