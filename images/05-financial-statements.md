# Image briefs — Chapter 5: Financial Statements

## Figure 5.1 — One factory, three statements

**Where:** Hook section.

**Subject.** A diagram with one centered icon (a factory labeled "$100M, March Year 1"), with three arrows radiating outward to three labeled boxes: "Income Statement: $6M depreciation expense (year 1)"; "Balance Sheet: $94M PP&E line (year-end)"; "Cash Flow Statement: $100M outflow under Investing Activities."

**Caption.** *Same fact, three different views. The factory shows up in three statements with three different timing and magnitude effects. None of the three on its own captures the whole transaction.*

---

## Figure 5.2 — The retained-earnings link

**Where:** Concept 2.

**Subject.** Three boxes vertically:
1. Income Statement — labeled "Net income (period)."
2. Statement of Equity — labeled "Beginning RE + Net income − Dividends = Ending RE."
3. Balance Sheet — labeled "Retained Earnings line equals Ending RE."

Arrows connecting the three boxes show the flow.

**Caption.** *Net income flows from income statement to balance sheet through the statement of equity. If the numbers don't tie, there's an error.*

## Figure 5.3 — Apple's four statements (illustrative)

**Where:** Concept 1, after the four-statements walkthrough.

**Subject.** A four-panel layout, each panel showing the title and a few line items from Apple's most recent 10-K:
- Income statement: revenue, gross profit, operating income, net income.
- Balance sheet: total assets, total liabilities, total equity.
- Statement of equity: beginning RE, net income, dividends, buybacks, ending RE.
- Cash flow statement: OCF, ICF, FCF (from financing), change in cash.

**Caption.** *Apple's four statements at a glance. Real numbers, real structure. Use this as a reference point throughout the project.*

## Figure 5.4 — Common-size income statement comparison

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** A side-by-side comparison table showing two firms' income statements in absolute dollars (left two columns) and as percentages of net sales (right two columns). Highlights where common-size analysis surfaces structural differences invisible in absolute figures.

**Caption.** *Absolute dollars hide structure. Common-size percentages reveal it.*

---

## Figure 5.5 — OCF and FCF over time

**Where:** Concept 3, after the OCF/FCF section.

**Subject.** Line chart for an example firm showing OCF and FCF over five years, with capex as the gap between them. Annotations call out years of unusual capex spikes and the resulting FCF dips.

**Caption.** *Free cash flow is what's left after the firm has paid for operations and maintained its productive assets. This is the cash available to return to capital providers.*
