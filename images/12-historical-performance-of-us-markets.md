# Image briefs — Chapter 12

## Figure 12.1 — Growth of $1 over 1928-2020

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** Log-scale line chart with four series: $1 in large-cap stocks, $1 in T-bonds, $1 in T-bills, $1 in inflation (CPI). Endpoints at end-2020. Logarithmic y-axis to make the differences visible.

**Caption.** *Compounding over a century. Stocks dominate; the spread is what the equity premium produces.*

## Figure 12.2 — Drawdowns of the S&P 500, 1928-2024

**Where:** Concept 2.

**Subject.** Underwater chart showing percentage drawdown from prior peak over the full period. Major drawdowns labeled with dates and peak-to-trough magnitudes.

**Caption.** *Long-run averages smooth across the experience of holding through these. Buy-and-hold means buying and holding through these.*

## Figure 12.3 — Equity premium across countries, 1900-2020

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** Bar chart from Dimson-Marsh-Staunton dataset showing average annual real return on equities minus average annual real return on bonds for ~20 developed countries. US near top; some European countries near bottom; Japan an outlier on the low side.

**Caption.** *The US equity premium is high by international standards. The premium is real but country-specific.*

`[verify]` from Credit Suisse / DMS yearbook.

## Figure 12.4 — Missing the best days

**Where:** Concept 3, in the worked example.

**Subject.** Bar chart showing terminal portfolio value: holding all 40 years (~$697K), missing 5 best days (~$432K), missing 10 best days (~$313K), missing 20 best days (~$X).
