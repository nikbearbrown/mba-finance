# Image briefs — Chapter 3: Economic Foundations: Money and Rates

## Figure 3.1 — The 2022–2023 Fed tightening cycle and its propagation

**Where:** Hook section.

**Subject.** Three-panel chart with shared time axis (2020–2025):
1. Federal funds rate (FRED: FEDFUNDS).
2. 30-year mortgage rate (FRED: MORTGAGE30US).
3. Headline CPI year-over-year inflation (FRED: CPIAUCSL, computed YoY).

Annotations on each panel marking March 2022 (start of tightening), July 2023 (peak rate), June 2022 (peak inflation), 2024 disinflation.

**Caption.** *One number propagates. The federal funds rate (top) drove mortgage rates (middle) and the lagged inflation response (bottom).*

## Figure 3.2 — The market for loanable funds

**Where:** Concept 1, in the supply-and-demand discussion.

**Subject.** Standard supply-demand chart:
- X-axis: quantity of loanable funds.
- Y-axis: interest rate.
- Upward-sloping supply curve labeled "Lenders/Savers."
- Downward-sloping demand curve labeled "Borrowers."
- Equilibrium point marked.
- A second supply curve (dashed) shifted left, illustrating Fed tightening.
- New equilibrium marked.

**Caption.** *Interest rates are prices. They move when supply or demand shifts.*

## Figure 3.3 — Inflation across countries, 1970–2020 (CPI indexed to 1970=100)

**Where:** Concept 2, in the CPI discussion.

**Subject.** Line chart with three lines:
- US CPI: 100 (1970) → 666.6 (2020).
- Switzerland CPI: 100 → 302.3.
- Japan CPI: 100 → 331.8 (with most growth in the 1970s, then nearly flat).

**Caption.** *Long-run inflation is dramatic and country-specific. The US price level rose nearly 7x; Switzerland's about 3x; Japan's by 3.3x almost entirely in its first decade.*

## Figure 3.4 — US business cycle: 1950–present

**Where:** Concept 3, in the business cycle section.

**Subject.** Real GDP chart with NBER-shaded recession periods. Annotations marking the longest expansions (1960s 106 months, 1990s 120 months, 2009–2020 128 months) and notable recessions (Great Depression 43 months, 2008 18 months, 2020 ~2 months).

**Caption.** *The cycle is real but irregular. Average expansion ~41 months; average contraction ~17 months. Recent cycles look longer than the historical average.*

## Figure 3.5 — Currency exposure decision tree

**Where:** Concept 3, in the exchange-rate section.

**Subject.** A simple flowchart:
- Start: "Does your firm have foreign-currency exposure?"
- Branch 1: "Foreign cash flows due in the future" → Transaction exposure.
- Branch 2: "Foreign subsidiaries with assets/liabilities" → Translation exposure.
- Branch 3: "Customers/competitors operating in different currencies" → Economic exposure.
- For each: brief one-line description and example hedge tool (forward contract, currency swap, etc.).

**Caption.** *Three flavors. Transaction is the most hedgeable. Economic is the hardest. Translation is mostly an accounting effect.*
