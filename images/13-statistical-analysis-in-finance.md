# Image briefs — Chapter 13

## Figure 13.1 — Arithmetic vs. geometric mean

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** Two-panel chart. Left panel: a calm return series with arithmetic and geometric means almost equal. Right panel: a volatile return series with arithmetic mean visibly higher than geometric mean. Highlights the volatility drag.

**Caption.** *Volatility drag. The more the returns scatter, the lower the compound return relative to the simple average.*

---

## Figure 13.2 — The normal distribution

**Where:** Concept 2.

**Subject.** Bell curve with annotated standard-deviation regions (±1σ = 68%, ±2σ = 95%, ±3σ = 99.7%). Below the curve, a smaller panel shows the *actual* distribution of S&P 500 monthly returns — visibly fatter tails than normal.

**Caption.** *Real returns are roughly bell-shaped. The tails are fatter than the model predicts.*

---

## Figure 13.3 — Correlation diagrams

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** Three scatter plots showing pairs of returns: $\rho = +0.9$ (tight upward line), $\rho = 0$ (cloud), $\rho = -0.5$ (downward scatter). Real-world examples noted alongside (e.g., "US stocks vs. US bonds: ~0.1 typically").

**Caption.** *Correlation visualized. Diversification benefit comes from non-perfect correlation.*

---

## Figure 13.4 — Sharpe ratio comparison

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** Bar chart showing Sharpe ratios for several investment vehicles: cash, bonds, broad equity index, individual stocks, hedge funds. Annotates which Sharpes are sustainable vs. likely to revert.

**Caption.** *Sharpe ratio is the workhorse metric for risk-adjusted return. Sustainable Sharpes above 1.0 are rare.*
