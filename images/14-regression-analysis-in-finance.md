# Image briefs — Chapter 14

## Figure 14.1 — Regression of a stock on the market

**Where:** Concept 1.

**Subject.** Scatter plot showing 60 monthly observations of one stock's returns (y-axis) vs. S&P 500 returns (x-axis). The OLS regression line drawn through. Slope (β), intercept (α), and R² annotated on the figure.

**Caption.** *The regression line is the best linear fit. The slope is beta. The dispersion around the line measures the firm's idiosyncratic risk.*

---

## Figure 14.2 — Beta interpretation

**Where:** Concept 2.

**Subject.** Three panels showing scatter plots for stocks with β = 0.5, β = 1.0, and β = 1.8. Same x-axis (market returns) for all three. Slopes visibly different.

**Caption.** *Different stocks, different sensitivity to the market. Beta captures the slope of that sensitivity.*

---

## Figure 14.3 — Security market line

**Where:** Concept 2, after the SML section.

**Subject.** Graph with beta on x-axis, expected return on y-axis. SML drawn as straight line from $(\beta = 0, R = R_f)$ through $(\beta = 1, R = E[R_m])$. Several stocks plotted as points; some on the line, some above, some below.

**Caption.** *The security market line. CAPM says fairly-priced stocks lie on this line. Stocks above are underpriced; below, overpriced.*

---

## Figure 14.4 — Cost of equity sensitivity

**Where:** Concept 3.

**Subject.** A 2D heatmap with beta (1.0–1.5) on one axis and ERP (4-6%) on the other. Cell colors show resulting cost of equity. Demonstrates the sensitivity range.

**Caption.** *Cost of equity sensitivity. The range across reasonable assumptions is the analyst's honest answer.*
