# Pantry — Chapter 11: Stocks and Stock Valuation

## Cold-open candidates

- **No contract puzzle** — bond has explicit cash flows; stock doesn't. Used as Ch 11 hook.
- **Triangulation example** — DDM, DCF, multiples produce different answers; which to trust? Reusable.

## Specifications

- **"Common stock"** — voting + residual claim + variable dividend.
- **"Preferred stock"** — fixed dividend + priority + no vote.
- **"Required return"** vs. **"discount rate"** vs. **"WACC"** — same concept, different framings; explicit in Ch 17.
- **"Free cash flow"** — cash to all capital providers after ops, taxes, capex, working capital.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **Why DDM doesn't apply to non-dividend-payers.** Cleanest single line of why DCF exists.
- **Terminal value dominance** — typically 60-80% of DCF total. Critical for Ch 18 forecasting honesty.
- **Why multiples inherit market-wide mispricing.** Bubbles inflate everyone.
- **The three EMH forms** — weak/semi-strong/strong; what each implies for active management.

## Worked-example structures

- **Gordon sensitivity** — 1% in $r$ or $g$ moves valuation 20-35%. Reusable in Ch 14, 17.
- **Two-stage DDM** — high-growth then stable-growth. Reusable in Ch 18.
- **DCF with terminal value** — full pattern. Reusable for project capstone.
- **Triangulation across three methods** — consensus + range. Reusable.

## Quotable phrasings

- "A bond's cash flows are contractual; a stock's are not."
- "DCF is a tool, not an oracle."
- "Disagreement between methods is informative."
- "The process is the work; the number is the byproduct."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **Sell-side bias** — Buy recommendations systematically over-issued.
- **Terminal value sensitivity** — small inputs swing big outputs.
- **EMH's empirical complexity** — markets efficient on average, not everywhere.
- **Behavioral finance** as qualifier, not replacement.
