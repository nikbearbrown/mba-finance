# Pantry — Chapter 14: Regression / Beta / CAPM

## Cold-open candidates

- **Two stocks, same return, different P/E** — the puzzle motivating beta. Used as Ch 14 hook.
- **Nike beta 0.83** — concrete real example. Reusable in Ch 15.
- **Walmart vs. Tesla beta** — defensive vs. growth. Reusable in Ch 17.

## Specifications

- **"Beta"** — slope of stock returns vs. market returns. Measures systematic risk. Not total risk.
- **"R²"** — fraction of variance explained. Reusable in any regression context.
- **"Alpha"** — regression intercept. Deviation from CAPM expectations.
- **"Systematic"** vs. **"idiosyncratic"** risk — captured vs. diversifiable.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **CAPM equation** $E[R] = R_f + \beta(E[R_m] - R_f)$. The bridge from regression to discount rate. Reusable in Ch 17.
- **Why diversifiable risk isn't priced** — investors can eliminate it for free.
- **Security market line** — graphical CAPM. Above the line = underpriced; below = overpriced.

## Worked-example structures

- **Nike CAPM** — beta 0.83, $R_f$ 4.5%, ERP 5% → cost of equity 8.65%. Reusable in Ch 17.
- **Sensitivity range** — ±0.2 on beta, ±1 pp on ERP → 9-11.5% range. Reusable for project.

## Quotable phrasings

- "Beta is the bridge."
- "Total return variability decomposes into systematic and idiosyncratic."
- "The market should compensate investors only for risk they can't escape."
- "Historical beta is your best available estimate, but it's an estimate."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **Fama-French multi-factor models** — beta isn't everything.
- **Beta instability over time** — Tesla 2018 vs. 2025.
- **ERP estimation uncertainty** — propagates to cost of equity.
