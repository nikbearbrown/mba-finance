## Specifications

- **"Profit"** vs. **"cash flow"** — different questions, different answers, both legitimate.
- **"Revenue"** — economic value created from delivering goods/services in a period; not the same as cash collected.
- **"Expense"** — economic resource consumed in a period; not the same as cash paid.
- **"Depreciation"** — accounting allocation of cost over useful life. Not market-value loss.
- **"Capital expenditure"** vs. **"operating expense"** — boundary case is where fraud lives. WorldCom canonical.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **Why the accounting equation must balance.** Definitional identity. Equity defined as Assets minus Liabilities.
- **Why double-entry catches errors.** Trial balance is the self-check.
- **Why straight-line depreciation is the public-company default.** Predictability + comparability + auditor preference.
- **Why deferred revenue is a *liability*.** Cash received, service not yet performed → company owes performance.
- **Why land is never depreciated.** Assumed unlimited useful life. Sits at historical cost.

## Analogies

- **Accounting equation as physics conservation.** "Just as energy is conserved in physics, claims are conserved in accounting." Limit: physics is fundamental; accounting is convention.
- **Depreciation as matching the bill to the meal.** Capex outlay should be matched to the revenue it produces; depreciation is the accounting realization of that.
