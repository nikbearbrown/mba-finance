## Specifications

- **"Net income"** vs. **"cash from operations"** — different by construction; gap is informative.
- **"Equity"** — book value, not market value. Apple's equity ≪ Apple's market cap.
- **"EBITDA"** — pretends depreciation isn't an expense; useful proxy but not a substitute.
- **"Free cash flow"** = OCF − Capex. The cash genuinely available to return to capital providers.
- **"Common-size"** — percentage representation. Lets you compare a $4B firm with a $400B firm meaningfully.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **The retained earnings link.** Net income flows from income statement → statement of equity → balance sheet.
- **The cash link.** Beginning cash + change = ending cash; ties cash flow statement to balance sheet.
- **Why expenses ≠ payables.** Expense recorded when incurred; payable recorded as liability; cash payment may come later. Reusable in Ch 6, Ch 19.
- **Common-size as scaling tool.** Reusable in Ch 6 (ratios are extensions), Ch 12 (long-run comparisons).

## Analogies

- **Income statement = movie of a period; balance sheet = photograph of one moment.** Limit: doesn't capture the integrated linkages.
- **Common-size analysis as zoom-out.** You lose scale; you gain structural comparability.

## Quotable phrasings

- "Same fact, three different stories, all true."
- "Each statement alone is a partial view; the system of four statements is the complete one."
- "Reading them is not optional; it is the foundational technical skill of equity research."
- "The redundancy is what makes accounting fraud difficult to commit cleanly."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **Buffett's EBITDA criticism.** Worth citing; calibrated skepticism on EBITDA usefulness.
- **GAAP vs. non-GAAP adjustments.** Open question; chapter gestures at it for later.
- **Internal-vs-external intangibles asymmetry.** Coke's brand is real but not on the balance sheet.
- **Window dressing at year-end.** Balance sheet is most-favorable date; analyst should compare quarterly statements.
