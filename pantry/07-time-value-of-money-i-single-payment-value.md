## Specifications

- **"Interest rate"** vs. **"discount rate"** — same number used in opposite directions.
- **"Compounding"** — interest on interest. Distinct from simple interest.
- **"Nominal"** rate vs. **"real"** rate — quoted vs. inflation-adjusted.
- **"Rule of 72"** — approximation, not exact. Good for rates between ~2% and ~12%.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **The master formula** $FV = PV(1+r)^n$ — works in both directions.
- **Why compounding matters more over time.** Each year you earn the rate on a slightly larger base. Reusable in Ch 12.
- **Compounding frequency formula** with $m$ periods per year: $FV = PV(1 + r/m)^{m \cdot n}$.
- **Continuous compounding limit**: $FV = PV \cdot e^{rn}$. Reusable in Ch 20.
- **Fisher equation**: $(1+i) = (1+R)(1+h)$.

## Quotable phrasings

- "Compounding is forward in time; discounting is backward in time. Both use the same factor."
- "Most of the action is in the second half of the time horizon."
- "The formula is precise; what we put into the formula is approximate."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **Negative real rates** — TVM intuition breaks when nominal rate < inflation rate.
- **Discounting long-horizon environmental claims** — heterodox economists argue traditional TVM understates future welfare. Open question; chapter punts.
- **Discount rate selection** as the genuinely hard problem — chapter flags it for Ch 11, 14, 17.
