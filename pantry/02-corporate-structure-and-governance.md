## Specifications

- **"Corporation"** — separate legal entity, not just "a big company." Three properties: limited liability, unlimited life, transferable ownership. Cost: double taxation (C-corp).
- **"Independence"** (of a director) — no material financial relationship beyond the directorship. Necessary but not sufficient for good governance.
- **"Stakeholders"** vs. "shareholders" — same word stem, different concept. Stakeholders = anyone with an interest. Shareholders = legal owners.
- **"Public"** company — registered with SEC, files mandatory disclosures, shares trade on a public exchange. Most C corps are *not* public.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **Why limited liability is the move that makes public ownership possible.** Without it, no rational stranger would buy a fraction of someone else's business.
- **The asset-substitution problem.** Once debt is issued, shareholders prefer riskier strategies than creditors expected. Bond covenants mitigate. Reusable in Ch 10, Ch 17.
- **The Delaware flip.** LLC → C corp conversion before VC funding. Standard script.
- **Why public companies disclose voluntarily.** Silence beyond mandatory disclosure raises cost of capital. Voluntary disclosure has economic teeth.

## Worked-example structures

- **Five-stage growth arc** (sole prop → LLC → multi-member LLC → C corp → public C corp) — clean way to show form follows function.
- **EDGAR analyst workflow** — six-step first-hour scan: 10-K → 10-Qs → DEF 14A → 8-Ks → Item 1, 1A → director list. Reusable as the "starting routine" template throughout the project.
- **Reading a proxy for agency-cost signals** — four questions: independence, compensation alignment, related-party transactions, shareholder proposals.

## Analogies

- **Articles of incorporation = a birth certificate for a legal person.** Limit: corporations don't actually die, while persons do.
- **Audit committee = the part of the board that doesn't trust the people running the firm.** Limit: blunt; many audit committees are fine.
