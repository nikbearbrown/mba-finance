## Specifications

- **"Mean"** — arithmetic vs. geometric; different answers for volatile data.
- **"Standard deviation"** vs. **"variance"** — square root relationship; std dev is the interpretable one.
- **"Correlation"** vs. **"covariance"** — same concept; correlation is normalized.
- **"Risk"** — standard deviation in finance, but with caveats about tail behavior.
- **"Sharpe ratio"** — excess return per unit of variance. The standard risk-adjusted-return metric.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **Volatility drag** — geometric mean ≈ arithmetic mean − σ²/2. Reusable in Ch 12, 15.
- **Portfolio variance formula** — diversification benefit comes from ρ < 1. Reusable in Ch 14, 15.
- **Why fat tails matter** — extreme events more common than normal distribution predicts.

## Quotable phrasings

- "A single year's return tells you almost nothing."
- "Standard deviation captures typical variability. It does not capture tail risk."
- "Spread is risk."
- "Diversification benefit comes from imperfect correlation."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **Non-normality of returns** — fat tails, negative skewness.
- **Sortino, Calmar, VaR** as alternatives to Sharpe.
- **Sample-size limits on Sharpe** — historical Sharpes from small samples are noisy.
