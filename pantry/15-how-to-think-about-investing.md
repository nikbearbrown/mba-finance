# Pantry — Chapter 15: How to Think about Investing

## Cold-open candidates

- **The $50,000 puzzle** — answer depends on time horizon. Used as Ch 15 hook.
- **2008 down payment loss** — concrete consequence of horizon-allocation mismatch.
- **Wong vs. Petrov** — risk-adjusted comparison reveals what raw returns hide.

## Specifications

- **"Risk capacity"** vs. **"risk tolerance"** — financial vs. emotional.
- **"Diversification"** — necessary; not sufficient.
- **"Active"** vs. **"passive"** — the empirical default.
- **"Investment Policy Statement"** — written plan; behavioral discipline tool.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **Why diversification works** — uncorrelated risks average out.
- **Why diversification has limits** — systematic risk remains; correlations rise in crisis.
- **Why fees compound badly** — 1% over 40 years = 25%+ of final wealth lost.
- **Why most active managers underperform** — fees + survivorship + skill rarity.

## Worked-example structures

- **Wong vs. Petrov risk-adjusted comparison.** Reusable in Ch 17.
- **Fee-impact computation** — $5K/year, 40 years, 8% vs. 8.45% net. Concrete.

## Quotable phrasings

- "The same $50,000 has radically different right answers depending on the question hidden inside the question."
- "Diversification is necessary but not sufficient."
- "The IPS is the antidote to behavioral mistakes."
- "Active investing requires a temperament most investors don't have."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **Some markets less efficient** — small-cap, emerging.
- **Some active managers genuinely skilled** — but hard to identify in advance.
- **2008 correlation crisis** — diversification weaker when needed most.
- **2022 60/40 collapse** — stocks and bonds fell together.
