# Pantry — Chapter 12: Historical Performance

## Cold-open candidates

- **$10,000 → $697,000** over 40 years in S&P 500. Used as Ch 12 hook.
- **Missing the 10 best days** cuts long-run return by more than half. Reusable in Ch 15.
- **2022's 60/40 collapse** — diversification fails when stocks and bonds fall together. Reusable in Ch 15, Ch 20.

## Specifications

- **"Equity risk premium"** — gap between stock and Treasury returns. Realized vs. expected: different.
- **"Drawdown"** — peak-to-trough decline.
- **"Realized return"** vs. **"expected return"** — what happened vs. what was anticipated.

## Mechanism explanations (reusable)

- **Why long-run averages hide drawdown experience.** Smoothing across 30 years averages a 50% loss in a single year into nothing.
- **Why missing best days is so costly.** Best days cluster in volatile periods.
- **Why ERP varies internationally.** Survivor bias plus genuine country-specific factors.

## Worked-example structures

- **Asset class table 1981-2020** — large-cap stocks 12.6%, T-bills 3.9%, ERP ~8.7%. Reusable in Ch 14, 17.
- **Drawdown summaries** — 1929 (-86%, 25 years), 2008 (-57%, 5.5 years), 2020 (-34%, 5 months). Reusable in Ch 20.
- **Missing-best-days computation** — $697K vs. $313K. Reusable in Ch 15.

## Quotable phrasings

- "Realized returns reflect what actually happened. Expected returns reflect what investors anticipated."
- "The historical record reflects one country over one century."
- "Best days and worst days cluster in volatile periods."
- "The forward-looking equity premium is probably lower than the realized one."

## Counter-arguments and limits

- **US survivor bias** — international data shows lower premia in many markets.
- **Falling rates 1981-2020** as a one-time tailwind — cannot repeat.
- **2010s-2020s may be unusual** in retrospect.
- **Diversification failure in 2008 and 2022** — correlations rise in stress.
